Handled — AI CRM

Handled vs ApplianceOps.

ApplianceOps is the only one of the five competitors built for one trade only — appliance repair, nothing else — and the only one with a price on the page: $35 a month. Handled is built around Max, an agent that runs the whole business by voice. Here is the fair comparison.

Handled is an AI CRM for small service businesses. Against ApplianceOps, its edges are voice control of the whole app, diagnostics from real manuals, and a client card that grows itself — plus a memory of which van drawer holds which part.

The short verdict

For a solo technician who wants a working system today at a known price, ApplianceOps is the most direct alternative Handled faces: $35 a month for the first seat, every feature included, no contract, and real appliance-shop workflows — home-warranty split billing, per-machine pricing, used-machine resale and full offline work — that none of the four generalists have. Handled's case is different: one agent that runs the CRM, the van stock, the parts search and the diagnostics — by voice, on any tab — with answers grounded in real manuals and marked confirmed or unconfirmed, a photo part ID, and a memory of which drawer in the van holds the part. ApplianceOps reads the model and serial number off a nameplate photo and decodes error codes; it does not, as far as its own pages say, search suppliers for a part with wholesale and retail prices, or remember which van drawer it sits in.

Where Handled is different

  • Voice control of the whole app — ApplianceOps answers calls and questions with AI; Handled lets the owner run the app itself by voice, on any tab.
  • Diagnostics from real manuals, with confirmed/unconfirmed labels — ApplianceOps decodes error codes, but no labels were found on its pages.
  • Photo part ID — ApplianceOps reads the model and serial off the nameplate; Handled also finds the part number from a photo of the part itself.
  • Van-drawer memory — Max remembers which drawer or box holds a part from a photograph; ApplianceOps tracks stock at the desk level.
  • Parts search across suppliers with retail and wholesale prices at once, and draft orders — nothing comparable was found at ApplianceOps.
  • Mileage from the start of work, and Reserve with Google — both in Handled, neither found at ApplianceOps.

Where the competitor is strong

  • One trade only — no plumbers, no lawns, no HVAC — and a published price: $35 a month for the first seat, $10 for each seat after, every feature included, no contract.
  • Home-warranty split billing and work-order parsing — dispatch emails and warranty sheets fill the ticket themselves, with claim and work-order numbers captured.
  • Used-machine resale with delivery, dual sign-off and a warranty clock — a whole line of business Handled does not cover.
  • Full offline work in the driveway — invoices, payments and signatures with zero bars, synced later.
  • Per-machine price book, route optimization, and a commission engine with payroll — built in, not an add-on.
  • AI Dispatch, a real phone answerer with a business number included, priced plainly at $65 or $95 a month — Handled's Sofia is the same idea, but Handled's price is still TBA.

Price

ApplianceOps publishes its price: $35 a month for the first seat, plus $10 for each additional seat — $45 for two seats, $55 for three, $65 for four, $75 for five — then $15 a seat beyond five. Every core CRM feature is included on every plan. The 14-day free trial (30 days with a referral code) needs no credit card and no contract. The only separate paid item is AI Dispatch — $65 a month for 1–2 techs (400 AI minutes) or $95 a month for 3–5 techs (750 minutes), prepaid, and not part of the free trial. Handled's price is still TBA, so no honest price comparison can be made yet: a solo technician comparing a published $35 a month against a price that is not announced yet will, fairly, see ApplianceOps as the cheaper known option. The pilot is the answer for now — the first ~10 companies use Handled free for ~2 weeks — and the price decision itself is still Badma's to make.
