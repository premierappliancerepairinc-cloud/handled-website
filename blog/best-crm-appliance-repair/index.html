What a repair shop actually needs

A repair business is not an office business. The owner is the technician, the dispatcher and the bookkeeper at the same time. Here is what the software has to do, in the order of what hurts most:

  • Take the call when your hands are in a washer — and book the job without you stopping work.
  • Keep the schedule honest — no double bookings, no jobs that drift past what was agreed.
  • Know what is on the van — so you do not drive back for a part you thought you had.
  • Find the part fast — with a real price, not a phone call to three suppliers.
  • Diagnose from the manual — not from a guess, and not from a web search.
  • Remember the customer and the machine — without you typing it in after every job.
  • Send the invoice the day the job closes — not on Sunday night.

How to judge a CRM before you pay for it

Five questions. Ask them about any CRM, including ours:

  1. Is the price published?

    If a vendor will not show you the price, the price is a negotiation. Housecall Pro publishes $59 to $299 a month (annual billing); Jobber publishes $29 to $399 a month. Workiz publishes no prices at all.

  2. Does it know your van?

    Housecall Pro has no native van-stock tracking. Jobber has no van-stock module either. Workiz keeps inventory on its top tier only, behind a quote.

  3. Does it find parts — or just store them?

    None of the four big tools was found to search stores for a part with retail and wholesale prices and draft the order for you to approve.

  4. Does it diagnose from manuals?

    Only Handled marks every answer confirmed — from a manual, with exact numbers and a source — or unconfirmed — an idea to verify by measurement.

  5. Can you run it by voice?

    Jobber's AI Voice covers field tasks in the mobile app. No competitor was found to offer voice control of the whole app, on any tab.

Why appliance repair first matters

Handled was not built for "home services" in general and then adapted to repairmen. It was built the other way round: a repairman needed an office that works while he repairs. The parts inventory exists because a van full of parts is a repairman's money. The diagnostics exist because a wrong diagnosis costs a part and a second trip. The client card exists because nobody wants to type in a model number after a long day. Other trades — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, cleaning — come next. Right now, this is a repairman's CRM.
