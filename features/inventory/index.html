Handled — AI CRM

Van inventory that manages itself.

Max knows what is on your van, writes off parts when a job closes, and tells you what to buy. No spreadsheets, no guessing, no second trip for a part you thought you had.

Handled knows what is on your van. Max writes off parts when a job closes and tells you what to buy.

What does Max track?

Every part on the van, per part — what it is, how many you have, and which job it went to. When a job closes, Max writes the part off automatically. When stock runs low, Max suggests what to buy, so the van is restocked before the week starts, not after a job fails for a missing part.

Why does it matter?

The most expensive part of a repair is often the second trip. A part you thought was on the van, a control board you meant to order, a pump you used last week and never replaced — each one costs a drive, a reschedule and a disappointed client. Van inventory that keeps itself up to date takes that failure off the table.

How does this compare with the competitors?

Inventory is a real gap at two of the four competitors, and a scope gap at the other two — checked 2026-11-11:

  • Workiz — Workiz does have inventory management, purchase orders and equipment tracking, but only on its top Ultimate tier, behind a sales quote. It tracks stock; it does not find parts across stores or draft orders.
  • Housecall Pro — No native van-stock inventory was found. Housecall Pro has equipment tracking (a record of customer equipment), and points users to QuickBooks Online Plus or the Ply integration for stock quantities.
  • Jobber — No van-stock inventory module was found. Jobber tracks material costs per job and has a Home Depot price and availability lookup for quoting.
  • ServiceTitan — ServiceTitan has inventory and purchasing modules with inventory-level recommendations, built as enterprise inventory for multi-truck operations.

Handled's inventory is van stock for a small shop, run by the same agent that books the jobs and orders the parts.
